Recommended Path Forward
Option A — Consolidate back to @onecommune (highest reach)
Post all Commune Podcast full episodes to @onecommune and add @jeffkrasno as a collaborator on every upload. The channel already generates 7,209 views/day from catalog SEO alone and delivers 11.7× more impressions per episode. Jeff's personal brand stays associated with every video through the collaborator tag. This is the maximum-reach path, trading social separation for audience size.
— or —
Option B — Keep channels separate, but fix what's broken at @jeffkrasno
If the team prefers separation, three changes are non-negotiable: (1) Fix CTR — 45% of episodes fall below 3%, indicating thumbnail and title problems. The top JK episode (Infrared, 6.31% CTR) and the top OC episodes (Qigong Day 1, 6.89%) both use specific benefit-led titles. The low performers use vague or obscure framing. (2) Stop publishing content categories with no proven Commune audience fit (political takes, business philosophy). (3) Publish the Commune Podcast name in every episode title — it's currently absent from almost all JK uploads, making the show invisible in search.
Quick wins — either path
Title formula: every top performer uses [Guest Name]: [Specific Outcome or Claim]. Infrared, Marianne Williamson, Dr. William Li all follow this — the low performers don't. Clip strategy: JK clips average 847 views vs OC clips at 4,005 — the teaser content isn't converting to full-episode views. Clips need a sharper watch-more hook in the final 3 seconds. Publishing cadence: Dec 2025 was JK's best month (47K views) driven by two breakout episodes back-to-back. Clustering strong guest releases rather than spacing them weekly likely amplifies each one.

45% of episodes fall below 3% CTR. YouTube's algorithm deprioritizes content with low CTR in browse and suggested feeds — this is a direct suppression signal. Industry benchmark for healthy podcast content is 3.5–5%.

Why YouTube Favors @onecommune
🔁
CTR compounds into distribution. Strong early CTR tells YouTube to push a video harder into suggested feeds, generating more watch time, earning more impressions — a self-reinforcing cycle. At 45% of episodes below 3%, @jeffkrasno never enters that loop.
🎯
Audience fit is already trained. YouTube knows exactly who watches @onecommune and serves episodes to lookalike audiences at scale. @jeffkrasno is still in the learning phase — explaining the 11.7× impressions gap despite similar content quality.
🔎
Search-optimized titles rank for years. @onecommune titles were built around evergreen search intent — addiction, longevity, grief, manifestation. YouTube Search works like Google: old episodes keep surfacing daily for free. @jeffkrasno titles skew conversational over searchable.

Bottom Line
"The data favors consolidation — but separation can work if the CTR and content-fit problems are fixed first."
@onecommune delivers 11.7× more impressions, 6× more watch time, and 13× more subscribers per episode in the same time window. The rate-normalized metrics show this isn't just a size advantage — @onecommune content genuinely converts better per impression served. That said, the gap is closeable at @jeffkrasno with three specific fixes: benefit-led titles with the Commune Podcast name, thumbnails that lift CTR above 4%, and a tighter content strategy focused on proven categories (health transformation, longevity, grief, addiction, consciousness). The current 45% below-3% CTR rate is the most urgent problem regardless of which channel the podcast lives on.
